## Who this is for

Replace a vague difficulty rating with evidence from unfamiliar practice tasks across four skills. This page provides a working method, not an individual exam or application decision.

## Key decisions

Choose the exact product or application task first. Keep official requirements separate from the original practice or checking activities below.

## Separate familiarity from ability

Use an unfamiliar permitted practice task before judging readiness. Note whether difficulty came from understanding the instructions, extracting information, organising a response or maintaining an interaction. A task repeated from memory does not give the same evidence.

## Make a four-row gap table

For listening, reading, writing and speaking, record one task attempted, the observed difficulty and the next repair. Keep objective answer checking separate from feedback on productive skills. Do not average unlike observations into a made-up pass probability.

## Choose the next repair by impact

Start with an error that repeatedly prevents task completion: for example, answering a different question or omitting a requested point. Plan a short original exercise and then retry a fresh task. More vocabulary alone may not address an instruction or organisation problem.

## Reassess the schedule

Compare two separate practice attempts under recorded conditions. If the same obstacle remains, extend preparation or obtain targeted teaching support. This page cannot rate your personal chance of passing or decide how a receiving authority will use a certificate.

## Mistakes to avoid

Do not treat a sample, practice result or another person’s experience as confirmation of your own booking, certificate acceptance or readiness. Record unresolved questions rather than filling in dates, fees or outcomes.
