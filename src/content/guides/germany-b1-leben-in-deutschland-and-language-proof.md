## Who this is for

Use this page if a settlement or naturalisation conversation has mixed up B1 language evidence with Leben in Deutschland (LiD) civic-knowledge evidence. It is for readers building a clean record before they ask the competent authority about their route.

The source hierarchy matters. National law can say which legal conditions exist for a general route. BAMF owns information about its integration-course final examinations and certificates. A receiving foreigners or nationality authority determines what it accepts for the reader's application. An authorised centre controls its own scheduled test, registration and result process. Keep the owner beside every claim.

## Key decisions

Section 17 of the Integration Course Ordinance names DTZ as the language test and LiD as the other final-test component. That describes two distinct test components in the integration-course context. Section 10 of the Nationality Act separately lists sufficient German language knowledge and knowledge of Germany's legal and social order and living conditions. These sources support a separation of evidence categories; they do not determine whether your local authority accepts a particular certificate for your application.

Ask the competent authority a route-specific question: do I need language evidence, civic-knowledge evidence, both, or an accepted alternative; what exact documents and formats are currently accepted; and how should they be submitted? Only the authority's answer counts: a provider page describes a product, and a centre issues a result rather than an answer.

If a test is needed, verify only that owner's product and the selected centre's current terms. Record the exact name on the certificate rather than shorthand such as “the B1 test.” Record every local claim with its source and checked date. This avoids accidentally using a result of an A2–B1 scaled language test as an assertion that civic knowledge has been demonstrated, or the reverse.

### Scenario: an adviser says “do LiD and B1”

Ask whether they mean the DTZ language test, an independent B1 exam, LiD, or all of these, and for which route. Then take that wording to the competent authority. The phrase may be useful orientation, but it does not say which document format the authority will accept or whether another evidence route applies.

### Scenario: you hold a Zertifikat Integrationskurs

Keep the certificate, the issuing source, issue date and any score documents together. Ask the receiving authority what it supports in your specific file, including language and civic-knowledge evidence, before declaring either condition closed. The certificate's issuance information is not the same as a final immigration or citizenship decision.

### Original asset: LiD four-owner evidence map and two-evidence matrix

Make a LiD four-owner evidence map for national law, BAMF exam information, the competent local authority and the selected centre. Add rows for Language, Civic knowledge, Other application conditions and Local procedure; then add columns for claimed requirement, document held, deciding owner, source URL, checked date, format instruction and unresolved question. Keep each answer in the row where the authority put it.

### Original asset: certificate transcription card

Copy the exact certificate title, test date, result wording, issuing body and number into a note. Beneath it, write “authority confirmation requested on: ___; response: ___.” This helps the authority see the precise document and prevents you from translating a brand name into a legal conclusion.

### Original asset: question-to-owner sorter

Sort questions before sending them. “What does LiD test?” goes to BAMF material. “Will this satisfy my application?” goes to the competent authority. “What is the local test date, identification rule or cancellation term?” goes to the selected centre. “How can I prepare?” goes to the exam owner’s official materials or an editorial practice plan.

[Integration Course Ordinance § 17](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/intv/__17.html), checked 13 September 2026, distinguishes DTZ and LiD and ties successful integration-course completion to B1 plus the required orientation-test result. Paragraph 5 permits LiD knowledge evidence for naturalisation subject to the naturalisation-test rules. Do not infer that any LiD score suffices. Ask the nationality authority to confirm the result requirement against your actual document before treating that lane as resolved.

### Route-specific handoff

For settlement, attach the knowledge question to the exact [settlement provision](/guides/goethe-b1-germany-settlement-work/) and its local instruction. For naturalisation, use the [citizenship language-proof file](/guides/germany-b1-citizenship-language-proof/) and ask about knowledge independently. An old integration-course certificate can prompt a useful question; it cannot decide every current condition.

Write one resolution per category: “Language: document ___, authority reply ___, unresolved issue ___.” Then write a separate line: “Knowledge: result ___, authority reply ___, unresolved issue ___.” If the authority accepts one document for more than one category, retain that specific instruction rather than assuming the same treatment for another procedure.

The integration-course final examination includes the Deutsch-Test für Zuwanderer for language and Leben in Deutschland for civic knowledge. Current naturalisation law also treats language knowledge and knowledge of Germany's legal and social order as separate requirements.

The two lanes do not lend each other evidence: a B1 language result proves language, and a Leben in Deutschland result proves civic knowledge. Neither result establishes application eligibility, and whether a particular result or alternative document satisfies your route remains a decision for the competent authority.

## What the official sources support

- DTZ assesses German language skills from A2 to B1 in the integration-course context.
- Leben in Deutschland is a separate civic-knowledge test.
- Under the cited conditions, a Leben in Deutschland result may be used to demonstrate the civic-knowledge requirement for naturalisation.

## Build a two-column evidence note

In one column, record the language level and exact language document the authority accepts. In the other, record the required civic-knowledge document or accepted alternative. For each item, save the deciding authority, source, date checked and document format.

Keep residence history, livelihood, identity, forms, appointments and other application conditions outside both columns.

## Working sequence

Open two evidence folders and label them before browsing. Read the national-law text and BAMF information only for their permitted scope. Then obtain the local authority’s current answer about your route. If it identifies an exam, read the product owner’s current page and ask the selected centre about local execution. Recheck the evidence matrix when a route, form, test date or document instruction changes.

## LiD evidence handoff

Before treating either column as ready, reread the exact authority request and the certificate wording side by side. If a term is unclear, quote it back to the authority and ask a narrow document-format question. This preserves the distinction between test information, evidence held and evidence accepted without guessing a result from a label.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Calling both tests “the B1 test”.
- Assuming the integration-course certificate settles every route requirement.
- Using an old description of naturalisation time rules from an exam-information page.
- Treating a general federal explanation as the local authority's final document instruction.
