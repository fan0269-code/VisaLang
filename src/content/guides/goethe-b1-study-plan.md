## Who this is for

Use this editorial planning template if Goethe-Zertifikat B1 is a possible, confirmed exam product for your own route and you need a realistic study structure around work or other responsibilities. It is for preparation, not proof acceptance.

Goethe owns the published B1 product information and official preparation materials. The receiving authority owns the decision on whether a language document fits the route. The selected centre owns its current local test administration.

## Key decisions

First decide whether you are studying the right product. Confirm the exact B1 product and your selected centre only after the receiving authority has said the proof may fit. Goethe’s official page supports that Goethe-Zertifikat B1 is a B1 German exam and links to its current product and practice information. Its official material, not this template, is the source for format and task details.

Second decide your available study capacity. Count realistic sessions, not ideal hours. The eight-week shape is merely an editorial way to rotate exposure, focused repair, timed practice and review. If you have fewer weeks, more weeks, care duties, illness or a changed date, change the plan rather than claiming a fixed number of hours is sufficient. Keep all time estimates as your own choices.

Third decide what to repair. A score alone is weak evidence. Use official practice material, label the module, identify a repeated error pattern and choose a next exercise. Practise on official or lawfully published material only; anonymous answers carry no format authority. If your accepted product changes to telc or DTZ, stop using this as a format plan and review the selected product owner’s material.

### Scenario: you have six weeks before a centre-confirmed date

Use the first session to sample each skill and build an error log. Select one or two recurring weaknesses, then alternate focused sessions with short maintenance in other skills. In the final week, use only current official-format material and leave time to read the centre’s own instructions. This is a personal plan; it does not mean a certificate will meet an authority’s timing or format rule.

### Scenario: authority acceptance is still unresolved

Do not book or switch products merely to make the schedule neat. You may practise general German or review Goethe material while you wait, but label the product decision unresolved. Ask the receiving authority for its exact accepted-proof wording; once it responds, choose a product and rebuild the plan around its official material and a centre’s actual availability.

### Original asset: study four-owner evidence map and weekly capacity card

Make a study four-owner evidence map for national orientation, receiving authority, Goethe and selected centre. Write the date range, confirmed product, centre status, sessions you can actually protect, official material link and one recovery slot. Under each session record module, task, error pattern and next action. The card is a commitment to a process, not evidence of readiness or proof acceptance.

### Original asset: module error log

Make four columns for reading, listening, writing and speaking. For each mistake record task source, task number, what happened, likely cause, one repair exercise and retest date. Examples of original labels are “missed changed time,” “ignored one writing prompt point,” or “did not respond to partner.” These labels describe study work; they are not official scoring categories.

### Original asset: exam-week boundary note

Write: “I will use the current selected-centre instruction for arrival, ID, accessibility, cancellation, result and certificate questions.” Put the source URL and date beside the note.

## Verify the exam before scheduling study

Use this page only after confirming that Goethe-Zertifikat B1 is a possible proof for your route and checking the current exam information. Goethe's B1 exam covers reading, listening, writing and speaking. Its official page and practice materials—not this schedule—define the exam product and current format.

The eight-week structure below is an editorial planning template: Goethe does not recommend it, eight weeks is not a requirement, and it promises neither a pass nor a score.

## An adjustable eight-week template

- **Weeks 1–2:** sample each skill with official practice material and record repeated gaps.
- **Weeks 3–4:** concentrate on the two weakest skills while keeping short practice in the others.
- **Weeks 5–6:** practise complete task sequences under time limits you take from the current official materials.
- **Weeks 7–8:** rehearse the confirmed format, review the error log and leave time for rest and exam-day preparation.

If your work, health, care responsibilities or exam date change, adjust the sequence. Do not compress the plan to meet an unconfirmed application deadline.

## How to adapt the template

Record the confirmed exam date, the available study sessions each week, the official material used and the result of each review. After each practice session, choose one error pattern to revisit instead of simply adding more hours.

Keep exam preparation separate from the application file. Practice readiness does not establish certificate acceptance, eligibility, an exemption or completion of other route conditions.

## Practice records and worked revisions

### Original asset: diagnosis and retest worksheet

Print this page and fill the blank row for each module, using the [official adult practice set](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/en/prf/prf/gzb1/ueb.html). Keep the original answer or recording so you can compare the next attempt. A diagnosis is an observation about one task; a mock is a rehearsal of the whole confirmed format. Use the official instructions for a mock, including its timing and permitted aids. Call a short exercise a short exercise; a mock follows the official instructions.

For writing, keep the prompt beside your draft and mark each requested action. For speaking, note whether your partner could respond to your proposal. Choose a repair that matches the error instead of merely repeating the same answer until it feels familiar.

### Original asset: writing revision — change a meeting

**Original VisaLang practice exercise.** This is a short teaching exercise, not an official exam task or a complete mock. Write to your course partner Mia: explain why you cannot meet on Friday, propose another time and place, and ask her to confirm.

**Initial version — deliberately incomplete:**

<div lang="de">
<p>Hallo Mia,<br />ich kann Freitag nicht. Treffen wir uns später? Schreib mir.<br />Viele Grüße<br />Alex</p>
</div>

**Problem to repair:** the message says you cannot attend but gives no reason. “Later” leaves the time and place unresolved. The reader cannot tell which alternative to confirm. The brief sentences are understandable; making them longer alone would not solve the task.

**Revised version:**

<div lang="de">
<p>Hallo Mia,<br />leider kann ich mich am Freitag nicht mit dir treffen, weil ich länger arbeiten muss. Können wir stattdessen am Samstag um 15 Uhr in der Bibliothek zusammen lernen? Bitte sag mir, ob dir der Termin passt. Wenn du am Samstag keine Zeit hast, schlag gern einen anderen Termin vor.<br />Viele Grüße<br />Alex</p>
</div>

**Why the revision helps:** the reason explains the change, the proposed time and place make it actionable, and the request invites confirmation or a counterproposal. The verb goes at the end of the clause introduced by <span lang="de">weil</span>. Both versions address the same person informally; the revision keeps that relationship consistent. This feedback describes specific changes and awards no official points.

**Transfer exercise:** write a new message changing a different arrangement. Invent your own reason, time and place; do not just copy the revised paragraph. Then cover the prompt and ask another reader to name the reason, the alternative and what reply you need.

**Self-check:** mark each requested action in your draft, underline the proposed arrangement, check the greeting and sign-off match the relationship, and check verb position in any subordinate clause. Record one remaining question for a teacher or proficient German speaker.

### Original asset: speaking revision — agree on a study session

**Original VisaLang practice exercise.** You and a partner want to study together. Agree on when, where and what each person will bring. This dialogue practises responding and negotiating; it is not the full speaking exam. A written script cannot assess pronunciation, fluency or listening under exam conditions.

**Initial dialogue — deliberately unfinished:**

<div lang="de">
<p>A: Wir lernen am Samstag in der Bibliothek.<br />B: Am Samstag habe ich keine Zeit.<br />A: Ich bringe Übungen mit.</p>
</div>

**Problem to repair:** A continues without responding to B's unavailable day. No shared plan has been agreed. Adding more prepared sentences would leave that central problem unresolved.

**Revised dialogue:**

<div lang="de">
<p>A: Wollen wir am Samstag in der Bibliothek zusammen lernen?<br />B: Am Samstag habe ich leider keine Zeit. Wie wäre es mit Sonntag?<br />A: Sonntag passt mir. Die Bibliothek ist dann geschlossen. Können wir bei mir lernen?<br />B: Ja, gern. Um wie viel Uhr?<br />A: Um 14 Uhr. Ich bringe die Übungen mit. Kannst du deine Notizen mitbringen?<br />B: Ja. Also treffen wir uns am Sonntag um 14 Uhr bei dir.<br />A: Genau, bis Sonntag!</p>
</div>

The library closure is a fictional detail in this exercise, not information about an actual library. **Why the revision helps:** B offers an alternative, A accepts it and addresses the place, both people take responsibility for materials, and B repeats the agreement so A can confirm it.

**Transfer exercise:** repeat with a different unavailable day and a new place. After the first attempt, swap roles. Do a third attempt without reading the script and ask your partner to introduce an unexpected constraint.

**Self-check:** did you respond to the actual suggestion, give a usable alternative, ask a relevant question and confirm the final arrangement? For voice feedback, ask a teacher or proficient German speaker to listen; the printed dialogue alone cannot establish how you sounded.

## Working sequence

Confirm the product boundary, then choose current official material. Establish a baseline without judging yourself from one result. Repeat a cycle of focused repair, short retest and evidence-based adjustment. Schedule a timed practice only when you can follow the official material’s current format. Recheck the centre’s instructions near the date and keep the receiving authority’s proof question in a separate file. This keeps preparation useful without overstating what it proves.

## Study handoff

At the end of each week, choose one change based on the error log: repeat a task type, use another official sample, or protect a recovery session. Change a task type, a sample or a recovery slot first. If the chosen exam changes after authority confirmation, rebuild the map and study card around the new owner’s official material.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Mixing Goethe and telc materials without labelling the different product — practice aimed at the wrong format wastes the sessions.
- Using unofficial tasks before checking current official practice materials — a stale or invented task does not match the current exam.
- Letting an unconfirmed authority or centre date drive the schedule — a guessed deadline can push you into the wrong product or timing.
