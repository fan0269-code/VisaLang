## Confirm the exact product first

This guide covers two general German products only:

- `Zertifikat Deutsch / telc Deutsch B1`; and
- `telc Deutsch B2`.

Do not apply this format to a school, business, nursing, medical, dual-level or other profession-specific product. A centre advertisement that says only `telc B1`, `telc B2` or `telc German` is not a complete product record.

Before preparing, save a product record with:

1. the complete exam name and level;
2. the current official telc product URL;
3. the delivery format offered for your sitting;
4. the selected telc examination centre and its written day plan; and
5. the date on which you checked each source.

Stop if the centre's product name does not match the official product you prepared for. Ask the centre to resolve the mismatch before booking or changing your study plan.

## Exam format: B1 and B2 are similar, not identical

Both products have a written examination and an oral examination. The written examination covers reading, language elements, listening and writing; the oral examination covers speaking. telc currently describes the oral examination for both products as a pair examination with preparation time.

| Component | General telc Deutsch B1 | General telc Deutsch B2 | What to verify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reading and language elements | Three reading parts and two language-elements parts share a 90-minute block | Three reading parts and two language-elements parts share a 90-minute block | Recheck the live product table and current official mock |
| Listening | Three parts, approximately 30 minutes | Three parts, approximately 20 minutes | Use audio from the exact official mock; do not transfer timing between levels |
| Writing | One part, 30 minutes | One part, 30 minutes | Use the exact product's current task and rating criteria |
| Speaking | Three parts, approximately 15 minutes; pair examination; 20-minute preparation | Three parts, approximately 15 minutes; pair examination; 20-minute preparation | Ask the selected centre for its actual candidate and day plan |
| Delivery format shown by telc | Computer or paper-based | Digital, hybrid or paper-based | Confirm which format the selected centre currently offers for the exact sitting |

The live telc product page controls the current product description. The selected centre controls the local timetable. telc's FAQ says the centre decides the examination date and currently limits the separation between written and oral parts when they are held on different days; that rule does not prove that your centre will split them or which part will come first.

## Scoring and results

The official B1 and B2 mock examinations reviewed for this guide use the same top-level weighting: the written examination accounts for 75% of the available points and the oral examination for 25%.

For both exact products, candidates must reach at least 60% in both the written and oral examinations; in other words, both the written and oral examinations require 60% of their available points. The reviewed official mocks state this as at least 135 written points and 45 oral points. Do not judge readiness from one combined practice total: record the written and oral outcomes separately.

telc determines the official examination result. Its current FAQ gives a general estimate of 4–6 weeks, depending on examination volume and processing work. This is not a guaranteed delivery date for your result or certificate. Ask the selected telc examination centre how it will communicate the result and hand over the certificate for your exact sitting, then leave contingency before any external deadline.

A telc pass rule does not decide whether a visa office, immigration authority, university, employer or professional regulator will accept the certificate. Confirm that separate question with the body receiving the proof.

## Build an official baseline

Open the live product page and download the linked official mock examination and audio for the exact product. Use that package as the format baseline because it contains the official task sequence, answer key, point weighting and rating criteria for writing and speaking.

Run the baseline under the published conditions:

1. Use the official timings and permitted aids shown in the current materials.
2. Complete the written tasks without pausing to study between subtests.
3. Record the speaking task using a partner when possible, but use only original responses.
4. Mark objective tasks with the official answer key.
5. Review writing and speaking against the published criteria; treat self-rating as diagnostic, not official scoring.

Do not reproduce, request or practise from confidential remembered live-test content. Use the official mock for the baseline and create new prompts that preserve the skill and task shape without copying protected test material.

## Create a diagnostic record

After the baseline, keep one diagnostic record rather than a single pass/fail guess:

| Evidence | Record | Training response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading | Wrong answer, text location and whether the miss was global, detailed or selective understanding | Repeat that reading mode with a different original text |
| Language elements | Grammar or vocabulary pattern behind each miss | Build short contrast sets, then apply them in new sentences |
| Listening | Missed cue, distractor or detail and whether timing caused the error | Practise the same listening purpose with new audio at normal speed |
| Writing | Unmet task point plus the relevant official rating criterion | Rewrite one section with a different topic and recheck the criterion |
| Speaking | Missed interaction move, unclear explanation or planning step plus the relevant official criterion | Repeat with a new partner prompt and require follow-up questions |

Keep B1 and B2 diagnostic records separate. Their task demands and official materials are not interchangeable even where the section names look similar.

## Turn the diagnosis into a weekly loop

Use a short repeatable loop:

1. Select the weakest observable subtest or productive-skill criterion from the diagnostic record.
2. Practise that skill with original material for the exact product level.
3. Re-run one official-style task under its time constraint.
4. Record whether the specific error pattern changed.
5. Move to the next weakness only when the evidence improves across more than one prompt.

Reserve full official-mock runs for checkpoints. Repeating the same answers can measure memory instead of exam skill, so use fresh original prompts between checkpoints.

## Registration and local execution check

The selected telc examination centre owns current local registration and delivery details. Before payment and again before test day, verify:

- the complete product name and delivery format;
- date, arrival time and whether the two examinations are split;
- fee, currency, included services and payment terms;
- registration deadline, identity documents and accessibility process;
- cancellation, transfer and repeat-booking terms; and
- how results and certificates will be communicated and collected.

Do not transfer any of these terms from another centre, country, product or sitting. A telc catalogue entry proves that a product exists; it does not prove a local seat or local terms.

## Common mistakes

- Preparing for a level label without saving the complete product name.
- Combining B1 and B2 timings into one assumed format.
- Using a total practice score while ignoring a weak written or oral part.
- Treating self-rated writing or speaking as an official telc result.
- Planning around the FAQ's general result estimate as if it were a deadline promise.
- Copying another centre's schedule, fee or registration rule.
- Assuming a passed exam is automatically accepted for an unrelated administrative purpose.

## Next action

Create the product record, download the current official mock from that exact product page, and run one baseline. Turn every miss into the diagnostic record, then confirm local execution with the selected telc examination centre before paying or fixing a deadline-dependent plan.
