## Who this page is for

Use this page when a settlement or naturalisation file might need German language evidence and someone has said “just take B1”. DTZ, Goethe-Zertifikat B1, and telc Deutsch B1 are three different products. The authority that receives the file decides which document, if any, it accepts. This page does not decide a settlement or citizenship outcome, and it does not publish a fee.

Start from the route, not the brand. Settlement questions belong on [Germany settlement permit and B1](/guides/goethe-b1-germany-settlement-work/). Naturalisation questions belong on [citizenship language proof](/guides/germany-b1-citizenship-language-proof/). The [B1 hub](/germany-b1-settlement-citizenship/) keeps the two files apart. Civic knowledge is a different column. Read [Leben in Deutschland and language proof](/guides/germany-b1-leben-in-deutschland-and-language-proof/) before you file a language result as the knowledge proof.

## Decision tree

1. **Name the receiving authority.** For a settlement permit, that is the competent foreigners authority. [BAMF-NAvI](https://bamf-navi.bamf.de/en/Themen/Behoerden/?typ=ABH) helps you find a foreigners authority. For naturalisation, ask the town, district, or foreigners authority which nationality authority is competent, as the federal portal describes. If the office is unknown, stop.
2. **Ask which route it is applying.** BAMF’s settling page (dated 1 March 2024) attaches CEFR B1 to some general paths, A2 to a refugee path it describes, and C1 to a shorter path it describes. EU Blue Card wording on that page is not the same sentence. “B1 for everyone” is the wrong starting point.
3. **Quote the language document it accepts, and quote the civic-knowledge document separately.** Residence Act section 9 (2) nos. 7 and 8 are separate settlement conditions. Nationality Act section 10 (1) nos. 6 and 7 are separate naturalisation conditions. A language result does not close the knowledge line.
4. **Match the quote to one row below.** If the authority wrote only “B1”, ask which exact document it wants. Do not pick a brand to fill the gap.
5. **Check the product with its owner, then the centre.** Fees stay with the centre or the local Goethe schedule. This page does not print them.
6. **Take the confirmed pair to the booking check.** If no row matches the authority’s words, do not book.

## Language evidence and civic knowledge stay apart

BAMF’s final-examination page describes Life in Germany as a separate sitting from the DTZ. It says the orientation-course pass is at least 15 of 33, and that proof of the naturalisation knowledge condition in Nationality Act section 10 (1) sentence 1 no. 7 follows the naturalisation-test ordinance at at least 17 of 33. Those two figures are not interchangeable, and neither figure is a B1 score.

The same BAMF final-examination page still contains an older residence sentence (“usually eight years”, “seven” with the integration-course certificate). Do not copy it. The five-year section 10 (1) line, the limit that BGBl. 2025 I No. 256 deletes subsection 3 only, and the instruction not to choose the English three-year sentence are on the [B1 hub](/germany-b1-settlement-citizenship/). Language evidence and civic-knowledge evidence stay separate. Settlement under Residence Act (AufenthG) section 9 is a different procedure from naturalisation under the Nationality Act. This page does not decide either procedure and does not copy fees. This comparison does not apply those residence conditions.

The federal naturalisation portal lists example language documents, including a BAMF language-course certificate and the DTZ certificate, and it says the list is only a selection. It lists the naturalisation test, and Life in Germany, under knowledge of the legal system and society. Example documents are not your authority’s decision.

Residence Act section 9 (2) says the language condition and the basic-knowledge condition are both demonstrated when an integration course has been successfully completed. That sentence is about the integration course, which on BAMF’s description includes the DTZ and Life in Germany. It is not a sentence that any Goethe B1 or telc B1 certificate replaces the knowledge proof.

## Fees and local terms

Do not rank these exams by price. The price is not a national figure.

- Goethe’s Exam Guidelines, section 6.1, say the fee follows the local fee schedule and is shown on the registration form or the centre’s exam description.
- telc’s support FAQ says the centre sets the fee and telc gives no figure. telc’s regulations say the same: each centre sets prices and registration conditions.
- A telc centre finder shows where licensed centres are. It does not show a free seat, a price, or a result date.

Record the centre’s own figure in a private note, with the URL and the date. Leave it out of any message that could be mistaken for a national price.

Goethe’s modular rules also describe when module results can be combined into an overall certificate, including a one-year window in the circumstances the Exam Guidelines state. That window is a Goethe product rule. It is not a rule that a visa, settlement, or citizenship file must use a certificate from the past year. Ask the authority what age, if any, it wants, and ask the centre which certificate form it can issue.

## What to ask before you pay

- Which language document do you accept for my route, and in what form?
- Do you accept a DTZ certificate at B1, at A2, or not for this route?
- Do you accept a Goethe B1 module certificate, a Goethe overall certificate, a telc Deutsch B1 certificate, or none of these?
- Which civic-knowledge document do you want, as a separate item?
- Which office issues that answer, and on what date did you check the page or the reply?

If any answer is missing, the comparison stays open.

## FAQ

### Who decides whether DTZ, Goethe B1, or telc B1 counts?

The authority that receives the application. A foreigners authority decides for the settlement route it is applying. A nationality authority decides for naturalisation. Exam-owner pages describe products. They do not issue the permit or the citizenship.

### Are DTZ, Goethe B1, and telc B1 the same exam?

No. DTZ is the scaled integration-course language test and can show A2 or B1. Goethe-Zertifikat B1 is modular under the Goethe Exam Guidelines. telc Deutsch B1 is telc’s general B1 examination with a written part and an oral part. Booking one does not produce the certificate of another.

### Does a B1 result prove civic knowledge?

No. Life in Germany and the naturalisation test are the knowledge proofs described on the BAMF and federal pages opened for this guide. Keep them in a separate column from DTZ, Goethe B1, and telc B1.

### Can this page tell me the fee?

No. Goethe points you to the local fee schedule. telc says the centre sets the price and does not publish one national figure. Ask the centre and save its answer.

## Common mistakes

- Treating “CEFR B1” as an acceptance list.
- Calling DTZ, Goethe B1, and telc B1 the same product.
- Using the BAMF final-examination page’s older eight-year and seven-year residence sentences. Section 10 (3) is repealed.
- Assuming every settlement path asks for B1. BAMF’s settling page shows other levels on other paths.
- Copying a Goethe marketing sentence from a product HTML page that did not open. Those pages are **needs-verify**.
- Using a centre finder as proof of a seat, a price, or a result date.
