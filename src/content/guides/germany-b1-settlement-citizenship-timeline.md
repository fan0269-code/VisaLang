## Who this is for

Use this page when you are organising a settlement-permit or naturalisation evidence sequence from dates you entered yourself or dates an owner has actually confirmed. It is for planning and rechecking, not for calculating residence periods, eligibility, processing times, certificate delivery or a standard appointment wait. The competent local authority remains responsible for the route and application procedure; a selected centre remains responsible for its local examination process.

Your timeline should show the source owner for every anchor. National law or a federal overview may explain a general condition. The local foreigners or nationality authority can state its current instruction, appointment and submission procedure. An exam owner describes a product. A selected official or authorised centre supplies current local transaction facts. A date from a source that cannot own it is a prompt to verify, not a planning anchor.

## Key decisions

The first decision is whether the route and proof question are confirmed. Before writing an exam date, record the local authority’s route-specific evidence wording and checked date. For naturalisation, keep language and civic-knowledge questions separate. For settlement, do not use a naturalisation statement as a substitution. If a required instruction is unavailable, mark the relevant timeline row unresolved rather than filling it with a general estimate.

The second decision is what counts as a confirmed centre date. A Goethe or telc finder can help identify where to check, but the selected centre’s current page or written confirmation is the source for its seat, registration, identification, fee, cancellation, result and certificate process. Do not infer a retake seat, a result interval or a delivery date from another centre’s policy.

The final decision is your personal buffer. You may add time for your own risk tolerance, preparation, translation or document checking, but label it as reader-set. It is not an official deadline, a legal calculation or a promise. Whenever an authority instruction, product, centre date or personal constraint changes, return to the affected row and recheck the source.

### Scenario: authority instruction exists but no centre is chosen

Put the authority instruction on the timeline as confirmed. Create an “exam execution” row marked unresolved and do not insert a fictional booking or result date. Compare only products the authority has left open, then contact selected centres. This tells you what is known without pretending the missing centre answer is ordinary or safe.

### Scenario: a centre changes the scheduled exam date

Replace the old centre date with the new confirmation, retain the checked date and mark every dependent personal plan for review. Do not move an application deadline or claim an automatic result window. Revisit the authority’s submission instruction and decide your own buffer again based on the updated facts.

### Original asset: timeline four-owner evidence map and anchor ledger

Create a timeline four-owner evidence map for national orientation, competent authority, exam owner and selected centre. Add columns for event, date or condition, source owner, exact URL or reply, checked date, status, boundary and recheck trigger. Suggested events are route instruction, proof acceptance, product choice, centre booking, exam date, result process, certificate process and submission recheck.

### Original asset: dependency arrows

Draw arrows from route instruction to proof acceptance, proof acceptance to product choice, product choice to centre booking, and centre booking to your personal preparation plan. Add an arrow from every item to final authority recheck. If the first node changes, inspect every downstream decision instead of changing only the calendar entry.

### Original asset: status vocabulary

Use only four labels: confirmed by named owner, published but needs local confirmation, reader-set buffer, and unresolved. Never write “guaranteed,” “standard,” or “usually” where you have no owner-confirmed evidence. This vocabulary helps a reader see whether a date is evidence, orientation, a personal decision or a gap.

## Start with two confirmed anchors

The first anchor is the competent authority's current application instruction, appointment or deadline. The second is the selected official or authorised centre's current exam and result process. If either is missing, mark it unresolved instead of inserting a default date.

This timeline does not calculate residence periods, eligibility or processing time.

## Keep separate settlement and citizenship tracks

Choose the track whose authority is handling your procedure. Copy the empty fields into your own notes; a blank stays unresolved. All dates below are reader-entered or owner-confirmed, not suggested waiting times.

### If a dependency has no confirmed date

Write the specific gap and the owner who can answer: “Knowledge evidence not yet confirmed — ask the nationality authority about my result,” or “certificate process not dated — ask the selected centre.” Do not generate a target date until you have the facts needed for your own plan. Any personal buffer belongs in a separate reader-set row.

If you change from settlement to naturalisation, open a new track and request the nationality authority's instructions. Existing documents can be recorded as documents held; their acceptance fields must be checked for the new procedure. Preserve the old track so a settlement reply is not mislabelled as a citizenship answer.

## Build the sequence from evidence

1. **Authority check:** save the route, required proof, procedure, source and date checked.
2. **Proof choice:** keep only the exam products or alternatives the authority has confirmed may fit.
3. **Centre check:** record the exact product, seat, registration, identification, fee, cancellation, result and certificate process.
4. **Reader-set buffer:** add only the time you choose after considering the confirmed dates and your own risk tolerance.
5. **Submission recheck:** revisit the authority's current instructions before submitting.

Do not copy another centre's result period, assume a retake seat, or create a standard appointment wait. If translation or certification is relevant, use the authority's actual instruction and a provider-confirmed date.

## Evidence record

For every date, store who supplied it, the exact source, the time zone if relevant, the date checked and whether it is confirmed or provisional. Note which event would make you recheck it—for example, a changed exam date, a new authority instruction or a missed appointment.

## Working sequence

Gather authority anchors first, then product and centre anchors, then add your own buffer. Keep time zones and dates in the form used by the owner. Preserve a source screenshot or reply only as a dated record and follow the current published instruction if it changes. Before submission, recheck the authority’s current procedure and compare the record with the actual documents.

## Timeline handoff

When a date moves, add a dated change note instead of overwriting its source. Identify whether the change belongs to the authority, the product owner, the selected centre or your own reader-set buffer. Then recheck only the dependent records. This leaves a transparent record without inventing a replacement wait time or a general certificate window.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Starting from an exam date before proof acceptance is clear.
- Treating a general result estimate as a guaranteed certificate date.
- Assuming a failed attempt can always be repeated before the target date.
- Using this planning sequence as a residence-duration calculation.
