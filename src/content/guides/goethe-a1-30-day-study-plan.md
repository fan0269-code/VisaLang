## Who this is for

Use this plan if your route and certificate question are already being checked and you are preparing specifically for the adult **Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1**. It turns official training material and the absorbed listening, speaking, writing, working-adult and safe-resource guidance into a repeatable month. Thirty days is a schedule, not a prediction: whether it is enough, which questions appear, and whether a mission accepts the certificate are answered elsewhere.

Goethe’s official exam and training pages distinguish the adult A1 exam from the young-people’s version and provide practice material for listening, reading, writing and speaking. Use those resources to calibrate the task format. Use the selected centre—not this plan—for current date, identity and test-day procedures.

## Key decisions

### Use one official format anchor

Choose the current adult Goethe A1 training page as your format anchor. Download or open a permitted sample, identify the four skills, and create a small practice routine around it. Buy only official or lawfully published material, and read a sample task as a format example. Other legitimate study resources can help, but the official material tells you what format you are preparing for.

### Keep all four skills visible

Each study day should touch two or more skills, and each week should revisit listening, reading, writing and speaking. A learner who only memorises a long introduction can miss simple information, spelling, form completion, or a short request. Keep responses small enough to review and repeat. The goal is controlled basic communication, not a polished script.

### Adapt effort instead of inventing an outcome

Use a smaller daily block when work or care duties are heavy, then record what was actually completed. If repeated errors remain or the official practice format is unfamiliar, extend the plan or review the exam date with your own circumstances in mind.

## 30-day study board

| Days | Focus | Daily evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–7 | personal details, alphabet, numbers, time and dates | one spoken introduction, one form entry |
| 8–14 | everyday exchanges, notices and short messages | one question/answer pair, one short written response |
| 15–21 | official-style four-skill practice | one timed sample block and error note |
| 22–30 | repair weak skills and calm rehearsal | revised errors, centre-instruction check |

For a normal day, choose a 35–60 minute block: short listening, reading/vocabulary, a few written lines, and one spoken response. With only 20 minutes, alternate listening/speaking with reading/writing; log the missed pair rather than pretending it happened.

### Scenario: you work full time and miss several evenings

A marathon does not rebuild a study rhythm. Restore the next small block, repeat one weak item, and keep the weekly review. A short recorded answer, simple form, and targeted listening task are useful evidence of practice. Move the calendar forward instead; readiness comes from the log, not from the date.

### Scenario: listening is much weaker than speaking

Use an official audio task after reading the prompt, listen once without pausing where the material permits, then identify whether the gap was a number, time, place, instruction or attention. Add original drills for that category. Loop a single answer until it is understood, then move to a new item. Pair the listening work with a one-minute spoken response so a familiar skill does not take over every study session.

### Scenario: you can speak but writing breaks down

Create short original forms and messages using changing names, dates, places and requested actions. Underline required details, write a first version without automatic translation, then check spelling, numbers and omissions. The task is not a predicted prompt. Use official training to understand the current format, then use your own variations to practise transferable control.

## Four-skill error log

Use four headings: listening, reading, writing, speaking. After each block, add one error you can name and one repair task for tomorrow. Examples: “confused 15 and 50—number drill,” “missed a date—listen once and note dates,” “left out an instruction—underline task points,” or “answer too long—give one clear sentence.” Keep the log private and concise.

## Weekly checkpoint card

At each week’s end, answer: Can I understand a short familiar message at the level I am practising? Can I provide basic personal details clearly? Can I write a short form or message with the required details? Can I ask and answer a simple question? Which two errors recur? What official or local action remains open? The card tells you what to practise next; it is not a pass prediction.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Treating 30 calendar days as an assurance of readiness — the board schedules practice; it cannot measure a pass.
- Using youth material when preparing for the adult product — a different format wastes the month on the wrong tasks.
- Memorising leaked or copied “real questions.” — memorised answers do not build transferable control and can breach exam rules.
- Spending every session on the skill that already feels comfortable — an untrained skill then fails under exam conditions.
- Switching resources whenever an error appears instead of recording and repairing it — the same error keeps returning without a named repair.
- Assuming a study plan answers local centre or mission questions — timing and acceptance still belong to the centre and mission.

## Four-owner responsibility map

Preparation uses four parties without blurring their authority. **National/orientation authority:** the Foreign Office can point a family-reunion reader toward the competent mission; it does not set a study outcome. **Competent mission or receiving authority:** controls the language-proof question for the application route. **Exam owner:** Goethe publishes the adult A1 product and official training material that anchors practice. **Selected centre and reader:** the centre supplies current local test-day administration, while the reader chooses lawful original practice, keeps a four-skill error log, and extends or reshapes the study board when evidence shows that more preparation is needed.

## A sustainable monthly rhythm

Treat the board as four short loops rather than a countdown. At the beginning of a week, choose one official sample or a small original exercise and identify the skill. During the week, practise the smallest repeatable unit: a time expression, a notice, a form field, a short request. At the end, review the error log and select only one or two repairs. This is particularly useful for working adults because an interrupted day does not invalidate the whole plan; it simply creates an item to resume.

Keep preparation lawful and calm. Official sample material is useful because it provides a transparent format reference. Your own prompts, recordings and error notes create the repetition that a sample alone cannot provide. Avoid “insider” material, promised question lists, answer memorisation services and guaranteed-pass marketing. They do not give a dependable account of your current skills and can distract from basic listening, reading, writing and speaking practice.

In the final days, spend a few minutes checking the real-world handoff: confirmed centre, exact exam, current test-day instruction and safe travel plan. Put any open immigration or booking question back into the appropriate route, centre, timeline or document record, then return to focused practice.
