## Who this is for

Use this page when you are preparing a German naturalisation file and need to separate a language-evidence question from every other condition. It is especially useful if you have seen “B1” in a national-law summary and need to know what to ask the authority next. It is not a naturalisation assessment, a substitute for a current local checklist, or an opinion on an exception.

National law, the local nationality authority, an exam owner and a test centre have different jobs. The Nationality Act is the source for the general legal framework. The competent authority assesses your individual application and local procedure. BAMF, Goethe or telc can explain their own tests. A centre can explain its current local administration.

## Key decisions

Under Section 10, the statutory language and civic-knowledge conditions are separate. The current text says that the language condition in paragraph 1 sentence 1 number 6 is met where the requirements of a CEFR B1 language examination are fulfilled, while the statute separately lists knowledge of Germany's legal and social order and living conditions. The provision also contains case-specific rules. Read this as a national-law starting point, not as a personal finding or a universal document list.

Your local authority must tell you what it currently accepts as evidence, in which format, and how it is to be submitted. Ask separately about civic-knowledge evidence, application forms, identity documents and the local channel. A federal portal can orient you to the process; it does not settle a local document dispute or decide an individual exception.

Only once the authority has identified possible language documents should you compare exam products. A product's CEFR label establishes what the provider says its product is, not that a nationality authority accepts that product in every form. Confirm the full certificate, any alternative evidence and the relevant date with the receiving authority before booking.

### Scenario: the authority page says B1 and “Einbürgerungstest”

Treat these as two questions. Put language evidence in one folder and civic-knowledge evidence in another. Ask the authority whether the terms on its page refer to your route and which documents it wants. Treat each successful test as evidence for its own lane only. Keep the page URL and date because the local procedure may change.

### Scenario: you completed an integration course years ago

The past document may be important, but this page cannot decide its use today. Send the authority the exact certificate name and ask whether it is accepted for your file and whether it needs an original, copy, translation or other verification. Book another exam only when the authority asks for one; a validity claim on a comparison chart carries no document instruction.

### Original asset: citizenship four-owner evidence map and two-folder label

Create a four-owner evidence map with columns for national law, competent nationality authority, exam owner and selected centre, then make a Language folder and a Civic knowledge folder. On every item write document title, issuing body, issue date, authority instruction it answers, format held and authority confirmation date. Keep a third folder called Wider file for every other statutory and local condition. This makes a missing category visible before submission.

### Original asset: authority-question checklist

Ask: “Which route and current local instruction apply? Which language documents are accepted in what format? Is civic-knowledge proof separately required and which documents are accepted? Which current form and submission channel should I use? What evidence is needed for any exception the authority says may be relevant?” Leave each answer blank until sourced.

### Original asset: claim-boundary note

For every note, add “supports” and “does not support.” Example: “Section 10 supports that the statute treats B1 language requirements and civic knowledge separately; it does not support that my local authority will accept my particular certificate.” The boundary line is a practical guard against over-reading a source.

## Your result: a naturalisation evidence file

Use the § 10 StAG result only when that is the naturalisation provision being checked. It leads to a [citizenship-specific checklist](/guides/germany-b1-settlement-citizenship-checklist/#citizenship-checklist), not the settlement checklist. A settlement permit application and a citizenship application are different procedures even if the same language document is being discussed.

The [current consolidated Nationality Act](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/BJNR005830913.html), § 10(1), numbers 6–7, § 10(4)–(6), and § 11, is the statutory reference checked on 13 September 2026. A B1 result answers the language lane alone; residence, status, exclusions and other conditions stay separate, and residence time is not calculated here.

### When the general language rule may not be your question

Section 10 includes special rules for children and certain former recruited workers and their spouses, hardship provisions, and illness, disability or age-related exceptions. Ask the competent nationality authority which provision it will assess and what evidence it needs. Other nationality routes remain [unresolved here](/guides/germany-b1-settlement-citizenship-checklist/#unknown-route-checklist); do not select § 10 just because you already hold B1.

### A concrete local starting point

[Berlin's naturalisation service](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/318998/) has separate language and knowledge document headings. It illustrates why you should ask about documents rather than only levels. Use it for Berlin procedure only; elsewhere find your city or district's Einbürgerungsbehörde on its official website. The current statute remains the legal source.

Copy this question: “I am checking naturalisation under ___. Please provide your current language, civic-knowledge and wider-file instructions. I hold a document titled ___, issued by ___ on ___. Which evidence format should I submit, and is there a separate assessment or alternative-proof question I should resolve first?”

Under the general route in Section 10 of the Nationality Act, German-language knowledge and knowledge of Germany's legal and social order are separate requirements. The Act describes B1 for the language requirement and also contains exceptions and special rules that an authority must apply to the individual case.

Whether § 10 is your route, whether a special rule fits and whether your document suffices are questions for the nationality authority; this page keeps them visible instead of answering them.

## Keep two evidence lists

**Language evidence:** save the authority's current wording about level, accepted document, certificate or alternative evidence, format and submission method.

**The rest of the file:** keep civic-knowledge evidence, identity, residence history, livelihood and every other document or declaration in the authority's current checklist. A B1 result does not complete that second list.

The federal naturalisation portal provides an overview. Your competent nationality authority controls the current local procedure and assesses your evidence.

## Questions for the competent authority

- Which naturalisation route and current instruction apply to me?
- Which language documents are accepted, and in what form?
- Is civic-knowledge evidence separately required, and what proof is accepted?
- Which forms, documents and submission channel are current for this authority?
- Are any possible exceptions relevant, and what evidence would the authority require to assess them?

Save the source or written reply and the date checked. Do not use another applicant's result as evidence for your own file.

## Working sequence

First identify the authority and current route information. Second, separate evidence categories before collecting documents. Third, ask the local authority to resolve document and procedure questions. Fourth, if an exam remains necessary, check the owner’s official product information and the selected centre’s local terms. Finally, compare the finished evidence folders to the authority’s current instruction before submitting. A study plan, booking page or centre finder can organise the work; eligibility, exceptions and outcomes come from the authority.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Treating B1 language evidence and civic-knowledge evidence as the same item.
- Treating an exam provider's page as a naturalisation acceptance decision.
- Applying a general federal overview as a complete local document list.
- Assuming an exception without an authority assessment.
