## Who this is for

Use this checklist after you know the relevant route and have selected an exact exam product or centre to investigate. It separates three document moments that are often wrongly merged: registration, test-day check-in, and the family-reunion visa file. Which passport, copy, translation, original or additional document your case needs comes from the selected centre for its two stages and from your German mission for the visa file.

## Key decisions

### Keep three owners visible

The exam owner explains the product and general format. The selected centre controls its own booking and test-day administration. The German mission controls the visa-file document instructions and decides recognition of language proof for the application. One document may be useful at one stage but irrelevant at another. For example, a booking email is evidence of registration, not automatically a visa document; a course record is not automatically an exam certificate.

### Register with consistent identity details

Use the legal spelling shown on the identification you will present, save the confirmation, and query the centre before paying if a mismatch exists. Do not infer an ID rule from another centre. Ask the selected centre what exact identification document it currently requires: a rule that works at one counter is not a universal identification or materials rule, and its answer belongs with the booking records.

### Build the visa-file list only from the mission

When you receive a certificate, compare its details with your own identity record and read the responsible mission’s current checklist. The mission, not the provider or centre, tells you what it currently asks for in the visa file, including any document form or additional evidence. If the page is unclear, ask through the mission’s official contact route.

## Three-stage document map

| Stage | Ask the owner | Save privately |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Booking | Selected centre: registration and identity instruction | Booking page, reply, confirmation |
| Test day | Selected centre: arrival, identification, permitted items | Current centre instruction |
| Visa file | Competent mission: certificate acceptance and document form | Mission checklist/reply |

Never promote an item from one column to another without the owner’s current instruction.

## Pre-payment stop gate

Do not pay until each of these has an observable record: the exact adult exam product, a path from the exam owner to the selected centre, the selected centre's current booking name and identification instruction, the fee and currency, the payment recipient or channel, the cancellation or rescheduling terms, and the way a registration result is communicated. An unanswered item that could affect admission, loss of money, or the route timeline is a stop condition.

If another centre's instruction differs from the selected centre's, keep both instructions attached to their owners and continue with the one your file names.

| Atomic check | Complete only when |
| --- | --- |
| Product and centre | The exact adult product and dated official path to the selected centre are saved. |
| Name and identification | The booking spelling matches the identification the selected centre says it will check, or the centre has resolved the mismatch in writing. |
| Transaction | The current fee, currency, payment route, cancellation and rescheduling terms are saved from the selected centre. |
| Test day | The selected centre's current arrival, identification and permitted-items instructions are saved for later reopening. |
| Submission | The competent mission's route-specific checklist is saved separately. |

### Scenario: the booking name differs before payment

Stop before payment if possible and ask the selected centre, through its published channel, how it handles the discrepancy. Record the exact spelling used in the booking, the identification you plan to present, and the reply. Separately, keep the mission’s visa-file requirements out of this centre conversation.

### Scenario: payment was already sent before a mismatch was found

A duplicate booking, a reversed payment or an edited identity record each adds a second problem. Save the booking confirmation and payment evidence privately, then use the selected centre's published channel to report the exact mismatch and ask for its correction, cancellation, transfer or other current process. Mark the item unresolved until that centre replies. If the correction changes the test or result timeline, recalculate the plan; it still does not change what the mission accepts for the visa file.

### Scenario: a centre email asks for identification but the mission checklist says nothing about it

Follow the centre email for test-day administration and retain it. The absence of an ID instruction on a mission page does not cancel a centre’s test-day requirement. Treat the two lists as parallel, owned workflows.

### Scenario: you have received a certificate

Save the certificate securely, note the issuing institution and date, and compare the printed name with your identity record. Then check the responsible mission’s current page or ask it whether the certificate form and any copies, translations or other evidence are requested for your route. Ask the mission which form of the certificate it wants before you rely on an original, a copy or a translation.

## Name-match check

Make a three-column comparison: booking name, identification name, certificate name. Copy characters exactly and add one source link or document reference for every entry. If any field differs, ask the owner of the affected stage.

## Source-and-copy log

For each document, write: what it is, which stage it belongs to, who instructed it, URL/reply reference, checked date, and whether you hold an original or copy. Send sensitive records only through the route the centre or mission publishes.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Treating a course completion document as the required language certificate — the visa file asks for the examined product, not attendance.
- Assuming a booking confirmation proves the visa-file requirement — registration and acceptance are owned by different parties.
- Copying an ID rule from a different city or provider — each centre states its own check-in rule.
- Ignoring a spelling difference until arrival at the centre — a mismatch found at check-in can block entry or force a correction on test day.
- Asking a centre to decide certificate acceptance for the visa file — the mission, not the centre, decides recognition.
- Sending sensitive records through an unofficial contact channel — passport or booking data can be exposed to an unverified party.

## Four-owner responsibility map

This document map keeps four owners separate. **National/orientation authority:** BAMF and the Foreign Office describe the language-proof framework and direct a reader to the competent route. **Competent mission or receiving authority:** specifies the visa-file instruction for the reader’s route, including any certificate question. **Exam owner:** identifies the examination and offers general product material without deciding a submission list. **Selected centre and reader:** the centre gives its current registration and test-day document instructions; the reader protects private records, compares names across documents, logs their source/date, and asks the owner of the affected stage when an item conflicts.

## A private review before each stage

Before registering, read only the booking row of your map and check that the centre, exact product and identity question belong together. Before travelling, read only the test-day row and reopen the selected centre’s most recent instruction. Before preparing the visa file, read only the mission row and reopen the mission’s current route-specific page. This sequence reduces the temptation to carry an instruction from one stage into another.

If you use a representative, translator, or family member for practical help, keep ownership clear. They can help you organise a file or read a page, but their summary is not the source. Save the original official link or reply where you can reopen it. Use secure private storage for sensitive items. Do not include passport numbers, booking codes, certificate serial information, or scans in a general planning sheet shared with people who do not need them.

When a page is unavailable, do not substitute a cached copy or an anecdote for current instruction. Record the page as unavailable, use the official published contact route, and wait for a response appropriate to the stage. The document map should make uncertainty visible early enough to avoid a preventable booking or submission error.

Use a separate folder or labelled envelope for each stage, but do not discard earlier evidence after a later stage is complete. A booking confirmation can help explain a centre query; a current centre instruction can help you prepare for test day; a mission checklist controls the visa-file assembly. Their value is different. Date each saved item and periodically remove access for anyone who no longer needs to help you organise it.

When a translated explanation conflicts with the official original, preserve both but seek clarification from the source that owns the stage. Do not ask a translator to decide an immigration rule or a centre to interpret a mission checklist. A document log is most useful when it shows exactly who supplied each instruction and when it was last checked.

If you receive a new instruction, add it rather than overwriting the old entry. Mark which one is current and why, so the checklist continues to show a transparent, dated decision trail.
