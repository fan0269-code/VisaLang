## Who this is for

Use this guide if you are preparing a German spouse or family-reunion application and somebody has told you to “get A1” before first confirming which route actually governs your file. It is for the applicant, sponsor, or family member who can identify the application country, relationship, and the sponsor’s present German status.

The first useful question is not “which course is fastest?” It is “which authority owns the language-proof decision for this application?” The Federal Foreign Office points applicants seeking to join a spouse to the competent German mission abroad and to BAMF information. BAMF explains basic German in the spousal-reunification context, but its leaflet also says the German mission where the visa is filed alone decides recognition of language proof. That boundary is why this page asks you to classify the route before comparing providers.

## Key decisions

### 1. Identify the relationship and application route

Write down whether the application concerns a spouse or registered partner, a parent or child, or another family-reunion route. Then record whether the sponsor is a German national, an EU citizen, or a third-country national holding a German residence title. Treat every rule as route-specific: conditions differ by category, and an exception depends on facts that only your mission can assess.

### 2. Find the competent mission, not a general overview

Locate the German mission responsible for the place where the application will be made. Save its family-reunion page and contact route. A national overview can orient you, but the mission’s current route-specific instruction is where you verify whether language proof is requested, which certificate form it wants, and what it says about documents.

### 3. Ask before paying for an exam

If you cannot tell from the mission instruction whether language proof is required or accepted, ask the mission or competent immigration authority a narrow question. Treat silence, social-media reports, a course advertisement and a centre’s opinion as questions for your mission. An exam centre may describe its own product and administration; it cannot decide a visa-file requirement.

## Scenario: the sponsor has a residence permit

You are joining a spouse who holds a German residence permit. Start by recording the permit category exactly as the sponsor can document it, then use the competent mission’s instructions for that route. BAMF materials say basic German is generally relevant in certain spousal-reunification situations, while also describing possible different rules and boundaries. That is not a finding that you personally need a certificate or meet an exception. Preserve the mission page and ask it to confirm the requirement and accepted proof for the route shown in your application.

## Scenario: the sponsor is a German national

You are joining a German spouse from outside Germany. The Foreign Office describes applying at the competent German mission abroad, which forwards the application to the foreigners authority. Use only the checklist published for your own mission. If your mission page points you to BAMF’s general information, treat it as orientation and keep the mission as the final source for your application instructions.

For this bounded route, apply through the competent German mission abroad; that mission forwards the application to the foreigners authority. The responsible German mission or competent immigration authority decides the visa route and its document instructions. A selected exam centre controls only its current booking and test-day process.

## Scenario: a friend says an exception applies

Record the fact your friend relies on, locate the mission’s current instruction, and ask whether that fact changes the language-proof requirement for your route. Book an exam only after the requirement is confirmed, and treat an exemption as something your own mission must state in writing.

## Mission question script

Use a short, factual message through the mission’s published contact route:

> I am applying for family reunion as [relationship] at [mission]. The sponsor is [nationality/residence category]. Please confirm whether I must submit proof of German language knowledge for this route, the exact certificate or document form currently accepted, and where the current checklist is published.

Add only facts you can support. Route these visa questions only to the mission.

## Decision log

For every answer, keep four fields: the page or written reply, its date, the decision it supports, and what it does not decide. For example, a mission reply may support “certificate requested for this route” but not the next local exam date. A Goethe or telc page may support a product description but not a mission’s acceptance decision. This simple split prevents a source from being stretched beyond its authority.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Treating every “family reunion” label as the same legal route.
- Reading a national FAQ as a personal exemption decision.
- Letting a school, broker, or test centre decide what a mission accepts.
- Booking a product before the exact proof question is resolved.
- Saving only a screenshot without the URL and date checked.
- Treating a delayed reply as confirmation.

## Four-owner responsibility map

This route map has four different owners. **National orientation authority:** the Federal Foreign Office and BAMF explain the general legal framework and direct readers toward the appropriate route. **Competent receiving authority:** the German mission where the application is filed, and where relevant the competent immigration authority, decide the route-specific language-proof and document instruction. **Exam owner:** Goethe or telc can identify an examination product and its published standards. **Selected centre and reader:** the centre controls its own booking and test-day process; the reader records facts and asks the mission’s narrow question.

## A careful handoff to the exam decision

Once the requirement record is complete, circle the one statement that actually answers the question for your route: a current mission instruction or a written clarification from the competent authority. Copy its limits exactly. If it says “submit proof,” it does not automatically name every certificate that could be used. If it names a certificate, it does not book a centre. If it says an additional question must be assessed, it does not mean the issue is already resolved.

Next, hand only the confirmed information to the comparison stage: the application location, route, exact accepted proof wording, and any stated document condition. Keep the mission link attached. This makes it easier to spot when a later centre page, seller, or well-meaning relative is answering a different question. Revisit the record if the application location, sponsor category, relationship category, or mission instruction changes. The safest outcome of this page is a bounded question with an identified owner, not a confident conclusion made from a broad overview.
