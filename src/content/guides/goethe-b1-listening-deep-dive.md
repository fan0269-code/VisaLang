## Who this is for

Separate missed facts, speaker intention and changes of position during listening practice. This page provides a working method, not an individual exam or application decision.

## Key decisions

Choose the exact product or application task first. Keep official requirements separate from the original practice or checking activities below.

## Read the listening question precisely

Before starting an official practice item, identify what it asks you to find. A place, reason and opinion are different information targets. Follow the selected exercise’s playback instructions during the first attempt and reserve extra replay for analysis.

## Use an evidence column

Write your answer alongside the phrase or detail you remember supporting it. After checking, identify whether you missed the signal, confused speakers or attached the correct fact to the wrong question. Do not reconstruct an answer from what seems plausible in real life.

## Practise a change of plan

Create a short original conversation where two people propose different times, reject one and agree on another. Ask a partner to identify the final arrangement and the reason for the change. Vary both values and speakers; this is a practice drill, not an examination prediction.

## Return to a fresh task

Compare the category of error on a later unfamiliar task. If you repeatedly catch individual words but miss the final meaning, practise connecting statements before increasing speed. Keep both task conditions and assistance in the log.

## Mistakes to avoid

Do not treat a sample, practice result or another person’s experience as confirmation of your own booking, certificate acceptance or readiness. Record unresolved questions rather than filling in dates, fees or outcomes.
