## Who this page is for

Use this page when you are about to submit a German settlement-permit file or a naturalisation file and you want a last check that the papers match the office that will receive them. It is a pre-submission review, not a legal opinion and not a national document catalogue. Settlement and naturalisation are separate bundles. This page does not decide a settlement or citizenship outcome, and it does not decide eligibility.

The route pages come first: [settlement permit and B1](/guides/goethe-b1-germany-settlement-work/) and [citizenship language proof](/guides/germany-b1-citizenship-language-proof/). The [B1 hub](/germany-b1-settlement-citizenship/) is the map. Family reunion from abroad is the [A1 hub](/germany-family-reunion-a1/), not this checklist.

## Two bundles, not one stack

Tick a box only when the authority for **that** bundle has named the item. An empty box means **unresolved**. Do not fill it from the other bundle, from a federal example list, or from memory.

### Settlement-permit bundle

The competent foreigners authority applies the settlement route. [BAMF-NAvI](https://bamf-navi.bamf.de/en/Themen/Behoerden/?typ=ABH) helps you find one. If it does not open, use the contact route your town or district publishes.

Residence Act section 9 is one settlement-permit provision, not every open-ended path. On section 9 (2), sufficient German is no. 7 and basic knowledge of the legal and social order and of living conditions is no. 8. Sentence 2 says a successfully completed integration course demonstrates both. A standalone B1 result is a different document. BAMF’s settling page, dated 1 March 2024, attaches B1 to some general paths, A2 to a refugee path it describes, and C1 to a shorter path it describes. EU Blue Card wording on that page is different again. Ask which path is yours.

### Naturalisation bundle

The competent nationality authority applies this route. The federal portal says to ask the town, district, or foreigners authority which office that is.

Nationality Act section 10 (1) states five years of lawful habitual residence, plus the other conditions in that subsection. Former subsection 3 is repealed. Do not plan from an older “eight years, or seven with a course certificate” sentence. BAMF’s naturalisation page dated 30 June 2025 says naturalisation is possible after three years where the special integration that page describes is shown. Only the nationality authority can say whether that sentence is yours.

Section 10 (1) no. 6 is sufficient German. Section 10 (4) says a CEFR B1 examination meets that condition, and it also states further cases. Do not apply an exception yourself. Section 10 (1) no. 7 is knowledge of the legal and social order and of living conditions. Section 10 (5) says this is as a rule shown by a successful naturalisation test. The federal portal also treats Life in Germany as knowledge proof in the way that page describes. A B1 or DTZ result is not that item.

The portal’s language examples — a BAMF language-course certificate, a DTZ certificate, named school or higher-education certificates — are a selection. The authority names the documents your file needs. BAMF’s naturalisation page says an integration-course certificate can show DTZ B1 on the language line and at least 17 of 33 on Life in Germany on the knowledge line. Those are two results.

## Fees and processing time stay on the official page

BAMF’s naturalisation page and the federal portal both display fee figures, and the portal also displays a processing-time remark and says its quick check is not a guarantee. This checklist does not copy those numbers. Open the page, then ask the competent authority which current statement applies to your file.

## FAQ

### Does a B1 certificate close the civic-knowledge item?

No. Language and civic knowledge are separate statutory items for both the settlement provision in section 9 (2) and the naturalisation provision in section 10 (1). Life in Germany and the naturalisation test are knowledge proofs. A completed integration course is a special case the statute and BAMF describe, and you still ask the authority whether your certificate is that case.

### Can I submit one folder for settlement and naturalisation?

No. The foreigners authority and the nationality authority run different procedures. Build the bundle the office in front of you named. Leave the other bundle closed until that second office names its own list.

### Should I copy the fee or the processing-time remark into my cover letter?

No. Read the figure on the official page if you need it, and ask the competent authority what it currently charges and how it describes timing. This page deliberately leaves those numbers out.

### What if a box is still empty on the day I planned to file?

Leave it unresolved and ask the authority before you submit. An empty box is not a guess, and it is not a reason to invent a document.

## Common mistakes

- Treating B1 as the whole settlement or naturalisation file.
- Putting a language certificate in the civic-knowledge slot.
- Using the repealed section 10 (3) residence wording that still appears on the BAMF final-examination page.
- Assuming every settlement path asks for B1.
- Reusing another city’s checklist or an old form.
- Copying a federal fee or a processing-time sentence into the file as if this guide had confirmed it.
