## Who this is for

Practise responding to another person rather than delivering a memorised topic speech. This page provides a working method, not an individual exam or application decision.

## Key decisions

Choose the exact product or application task first. Keep official requirements separate from the original practice or checking activities below.

## Start with an original shared decision

Imagine planning a small study meeting with a partner. Each person proposes a place, time and activity, then gives a reason. These are editorial practice prompts, not a list of upcoming examination topics.

## Introduce a real disagreement

Give the partner a different constraint, such as a changed work shift. Listen, acknowledge the constraint and suggest an alternative. Ask the partner to summarise the final agreement so you can see whether both people understood the same decision.

## Review interaction separately

Record whether each person asked a question, responded to the answer, gave a reason and helped reach a conclusion. With consent, replay a private recording and choose one breakdown to repair. Do not convert this informal checklist into an official score.

## Use the official format anchor

Compare your practice routine with the current adult training material. Include unfamiliar follow-up questions rather than rehearsing one fixed monologue. If the conversation only works with your usual partner, change roles or details to test whether the skill transfers.

## Mistakes to avoid

Do not treat a sample, practice result or another person’s experience as confirmation of your own booking, certificate acceptance or readiness. Record unresolved questions rather than filling in dates, fees or outcomes.
