## Start with the programme, not the exam

Start with the exact programme. The target university and programme decide how their current result pattern, document, stage, and application procedure apply to you. Open the programme's admissions or International Office page before choosing an exam. Record the exact programme name, application cycle, page URL, and the date you checked it.

[uni-assist](https://www.uni-assist.de/en/how-to-apply/assemble-your-documents/language-certificates/) lists TestDaF among the German-language certificates it says are accepted by all universities, while also stating that required proficiency levels can vary by university and course. The [TestDaF university information page](https://www.testdaf.de/de/hochschulen/der-testdaf-und-hochschulen/nachweis-der-deutschkenntnisse-fuer-das-studium/) cites the current RO-DT framework: TDN 4 in all four components is valid proof for unrestricted admission or enrolment, while universities may apply differentiated component requirements for a programme.

Those framework statements establish the general recognition boundary. They do not replace the named programme's current implementation.

## What to verify on the programme page

Look for the programme's current instructions on:

- the language of instruction and any language proof it requires;
- how it lists TestDaF and the result pattern it requires;
- any stated result requirement, including component requirements where the programme gives them;
- required application materials and the application route; and
- the programme's current application and enrolment instructions.

An English-taught course can still require proof of German. Do not infer the programme's requirement from its teaching language, another university, or a general Germany overview.

## Keep a programme-specific evidence record

For each programme, save one dated record with:

- the exact university, degree title, and intake or application cycle;
- the official page that states the language-proof requirement;
- the accepted proof and result wording exactly as published;
- whether the proof is needed at application, admission, or enrolment, if the page distinguishes those stages; and
- the official contact used to resolve any unclear wording.

Do not merge requirements from different programmes into one assumed rule. The general recognition framework does not establish how one programme applies its current result, document, stage, or submission requirements.

## What the general official sources can and cannot do

[uni-assist](https://www.uni-assist.de/en/how-to-apply/assemble-your-documents/language-certificates/) supports the general certificate-list and university/course-level variation statements above. [DAAD](https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/requirements/overview/) directs applicants to the higher education institution and its International Office because the institution makes the final decision and current application details can differ.

Use those sources to understand the general framework and application path. They cannot confirm your programme's current component pattern, document form, submission stage, application route, deadline, or individual admission result.

## Use TestDaF sources for TestDaF information

The [TestDaF university information page](https://www.testdaf.de/de/hochschulen/der-testdaf-und-hochschulen/nachweis-der-deutschkenntnisse-fuer-das-studium/) explains the TestDaF framework, its TDN levels, and the RO-DT recognition statement. Use it for those provider and framework facts, then use the named programme page for the programme's current implementation.

## Next action

1. Open the current admissions page for each programme on your shortlist.
2. Save the accepted-language-proof wording, any stated result requirement, the application materials, and the checked date.
3. If the page is unclear, ask the university's International Office before registering for TestDaF.
4. After the programme requirement is confirmed, use the [official TestDaF site](https://www.testdaf.de/de/) for examination information and the selected official test centre for its local registration terms.
