## Who this is for

Use this comparison when a German mission or authority has told you that a language certificate is relevant to your family-reunion file, or when you need to ask it which exact proof it accepts. It compares two specific adult exams: **Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1** (Goethe-Institut) and **Start Deutsch 1 / telc Deutsch A1** (telc). It does not rank providers, quote local prices, or claim either certificate is accepted in every case.

## Key decisions

### Read the product table first

The two products are close enough that exam structure alone rarely decides the choice: both are CEFR A1 adult exams with a written and an oral section. The differences that actually matter are which exact product your mission accepts and which official centre you can reach and afford. Open the source-reviewed product table in the "Detailed explanation" section for the exact durations, pass marks and retake rules, then come back here for the acceptance and local-execution checks.

### Acceptance comes before comparison

The BAMF leaflet says the mission where a visa is filed decides recognition of language proof. Record the authority’s exact wording before you compare: if it names a certificate, copy it; if it gives only a category, ask which exact product and document form are accepted.

### Compare the adult product, not the A1 label

Goethe lists **Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1** for adults and separately lists a youth examination. telc’s official catalogue includes more than one German A1-labelled product. “A1,” a course enrolment, a mock test, or a school’s marketing label is insufficient for a visa-file comparison. Your record should name the exact product, the official provider page, and the centre that offers it.

### Compare local execution only after both product checks

An official centre can then tell you its current availability, registration channel, payment terms, identification instruction, result process, certificate collection or delivery process, and cancellation or rescheduling terms. Those are local facts. Keep them in a dated record rather than using another country’s fee, a forum post, or an old search result.

### Scenario: both products appear on local websites

Two nearby centres advertise “A1,” one for Goethe and one for telc. Do not choose by travel distance yet. First ask the responsible mission whether each exact product is accepted for the route. Then reach each centre through the provider’s official route and ask its own current terms. The resulting choice may still depend on an unavailable seat, but that is a centre question, not proof that the certificate is acceptable.

### Scenario: the mission names a provider but the centre offers a different A1 exam

The mission message refers to a provider while the centre’s booking page shows an A1 product with a different full name. Pause. Send the full product name to the mission or authority and request confirmation. A similar level does not make two products interchangeable. Keep the centre’s page as evidence of what it offers, not as evidence that the mission will accept it.

### Scenario: a lower price is advertised elsewhere

Keep a price, booking calendar or result expectation with the centre that supplied it. Prices and administrative terms are owned by the selected centre and can change. Compare only the terms supplied by centres you can actually use after the authority and product checks are complete.

## Provider and centre matrix

Make a row for each viable option: provider, exact exam, official-centre link, place, earliest confirmed booking route, current terms link, contact date, and unresolved question. Add no ranking score; use the matrix to show which evidence is still missing.

## Acceptance confirmation script

> I am applying for [family-reunion route] at [mission]. May I submit [full exact Goethe/telc product name] as language proof for my application? Please point me to the current official instruction and specify any required certificate form.

Use the answer only for the product and route it addresses. A new route, country, product, or document form needs its own check.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Comparing a Goethe adult exam with a youth product or an unspecified telc A1 label.
- Treating the provider’s recognition statement as the mission’s visa acceptance decision.
- Letting fee, commute, or a class package decide before acceptance is clear.
- Assuming the provider owns a centre’s current seat, ID, timing, or refund terms.
- Treating a practice certificate or course completion paper as the examined product.
- Promising a result, a visa outcome, or a deadline from general information.

## Four-party responsibility map

This comparison has four parties with deliberately narrow jobs. **National/orientation authority:** BAMF and the Federal Foreign Office explain the broad spousal-reunification context, but do not rank exams. **Competent mission or receiving authority:** confirms the proof that fits the reader’s route and document instruction. **Exam owner:** Goethe or telc identifies the full examination product and provider-controlled information. **Selected centre and reader:** a centre supplies current local administration facts, while the reader compares only dated evidence and records a personal practical choice without presenting it as universal acceptance, price, speed, or legal advice.

## How to make a comparison that stays useful

Read the comparison record from left to right. First remove any product that the competent authority has not confirmed or that cannot be identified exactly. Then remove any product for which you cannot reach an official local centre. Only after those two checks should you request local administration information. This order prevents a cheap course bundle, a familiar brand, or an early advertised session from becoming the hidden decision-maker.

When two verified options remain, compare the dated evidence you hold: whether each centre confirms the exact product, its registration path, its test-day instructions, and the way it describes results and certificates. You may choose based on your documented practical constraints, but write the reason as a reader decision rather than an official claim. For example: “I selected the only centre whose exact product and local registration channel I could verify today.” That is very different from claiming it is faster, universally accepted, or better.

If a provider, centre, or authority updates its page, revisit only the affected row and date the new evidence.

Before closing the record, read each source boundary aloud: the mission can answer whether a proof fits a route, the provider can identify its examination, and the centre can describe its present administration. If an answer seems to cross those boundaries, ask the original owner again. This is especially important where an agent or course seller presents a convenient package: it can describe a service, yet acceptability stays an authority question, product identity an exam-owner question, and availability and process centre questions. Keep a separate line for the date you need to review the comparison, such as after a new mission instruction or before payment.

Keep a rejected or unavailable option in the record. Mark why it stopped: product not confirmed, no official local route found, centre terms unresolved, or reader constraint. This preserves the evidence trail and helps prevent the same option being reconsidered later on the strength of an advertisement alone.
