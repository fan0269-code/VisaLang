## Who this is for

Use this route note if you are considering a German settlement permit and have heard that “B1” may matter. It is for a reader who can identify an actual residence route, or can ask the local foreigners authority to identify it. It is not a verdict on whether you qualify. In particular, it cannot decide whether a statutory exception, a previous qualification, a different residence title, or an individual document is relevant.

Keep four owners in view. National law can describe a general route and its conditions. The competent local foreigners authority applies the route to your application and tells you its procedure. An exam owner describes its own test product. A selected official or authorised centre controls the local purchase and test-day process. A helpful answer from one owner is not automatically an answer from another.

## Key decisions

The first decision is not “Goethe or telc?” It is whether the authority has identified the residence provision and language-evidence question that actually belong to your file. Section 9 of the Residence Act is an official national-law source for the general settlement permit route; it lists sufficient German-language knowledge among multiple conditions. Put the provision, page URL, date and authority contact in your record.

The second decision is what the authority means by acceptable proof. Ask for the level, the certificate or alternative evidence, whether particular modules or originals are needed, and how the evidence must be submitted. Confirm the exact level and document the authority means before comparing any product. If the reply is verbal, make a dated note and ask where the current instruction is published.

The third decision is an exam product only after that answer. Goethe describes Goethe-Zertifikat B1 as its B1 exam and provides product information and practice material. The selected centre can then answer its own current dates, price, identity, cancellation, result and certificate questions.

### Scenario: Section 9 is named in a local instruction

You have a current local instruction that names Section 9 and asks for language evidence, but it does not name an exam. Save that wording before comparing products. Send the authority a short question asking which document types and formats it currently accepts for this route. Until the answer arrives, practise only with material you can use regardless of brand; book a seat once the authority has named the proof it expects.

### Scenario: a colleague says their Goethe certificate worked

Your colleague may have had a different residence title, local authority, date, or evidence package. Their experience can help you formulate questions, but it is not a current decision for you. Ask your authority for the route-specific answer, then ask the chosen centre for transaction terms. Record the colleague's claim as an anecdote, not as evidence.

### Original asset: route-to-proof question card

Copy this into your notes: “My route/provision shown by the authority is: ___. Please confirm the current language-evidence requirement, accepted document types, required format, and submission method for this route. I am not asking for an eligibility decision.” Attach the source URL and the date sent.

### Original asset: four-owner evidence map

Make four columns labelled national law, competent local authority, exam owner and selected centre. In each row enter the claim, source URL, date checked, owner and boundary. For example, “Section 9 lists language knowledge” belongs in national law; “this certificate format is accepted for my application” belongs only in the local-authority column once confirmed.

### Original asset: unresolved-item register

For each unanswered point, write the question, who can answer it, the date asked, evidence received and next recheck date. Use “unresolved” rather than a guessed yes or no. This prevents an exam date, an online forum post or a general overview from silently becoming a legal conclusion.

## Start with the legal route, not the exam brand

### A local checklist is tied to a place

[Berlin's general settlement service](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/121864/) separates language and legal/social knowledge and directs certain groups to different services. It is a Berlin procedural example, not a document list for every German city. If you live elsewhere, find the Ausländerbehörde on your city or district's official website and ask for its page for the exact provision. Save the local URL beside the statutory reference.

### Copyable settlement enquiry

“The provision on my current residence title is ___. The settlement service I found names ___. Please confirm whether that is the appropriate service and provide your current checklist. Which language and knowledge documents and formats should I prepare? Which residence, employment, contribution and other records should I provide for your assessment?”

Send personal identity documents only through the authority's own contact or submission route.

Germany has more than one settlement-permit route. Section 9 of the Residence Act is one route and lists language knowledge among several separate conditions. Other routes may rely on different provisions or exceptions. A general B1 mention answers the provision question for nobody: the provision your authority named decides the level, the proof and the certificate, and each route answer stays in its own file.

First identify the provision or application category shown by your competent local foreigners authority. Keep its current instruction, the URL or written reply, and the date checked.

## Keep three decisions separate

1. **Route and eligibility:** the competent authority identifies the applicable route and assesses the complete file.
2. **Language-proof acceptance:** the same authority confirms the exact level, certificate, modules or document format it will accept for that route.
3. **Exam execution:** the exam owner and selected official or authorised centre control the product, booking, identification, fee, cancellation, result and certificate process.

Goethe, telc or an integration-course provider can explain an exam product. They cannot decide a settlement-permit application.

## What to save before comparing exams

- The route name and statutory provision shown by the authority.
- The authority's exact wording about language knowledge and accepted proof.
- Any stated document-format, original, copy or verification requirement.
- The source or written reply and the date you checked it.
- Unanswered questions marked for confirmation rather than filled with assumptions.

Keep each route answer in its own file.

Civic-knowledge evidence is a separate lane; use the [Leben in Deutschland and DTZ evidence lanes](/guides/germany-b1-leben-in-deutschland-and-language-proof/) to tell the two documents apart.

## Working sequence

Start with an authority record, not a shopping list. Capture the authority name, your route reference, current link or form, the exact language wording, and its checked date. Next, make a separate proof record containing only documents the authority has said may fit. Then compare the remaining exam products on their owners' official pages. Finally, contact the selected centre for the exact local transaction. Recheck the authority instruction before submission because the authority—not this page or the centre—assesses the complete file.

The sequence also protects against a common planning error: treating language proof as the whole application. Keep language evidence separate from residence history, livelihood, pension, housing, identity, declarations and any other conditions. The applicable authority decides what is needed in the individual case. This page gives you a way to keep that decision visible; it does not replace it.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Treating Section 9 as the rule for every settlement route.
- Treating a B1 result as proof that livelihood, pension, housing or other conditions are satisfied.
- Booking a convenient exam before the authority confirms the accepted proof.
- Using an exam provider's product description as an acceptance decision.

## Next action

When you have a written or published local answer on possible proof, use the [DTZ, Goethe B1 and telc B1 comparison](/guides/goethe-b1-vs-telc-b1/) to compare only those products. If the route itself is unclear, pause and use BAMF-NAvI to locate the competent authority rather than treating a test provider as the deciding office. Before booking, submitting or relying on an old reply, recheck the competent authority's current instruction.
