## Who this page is for

Use this page after the [language-requirement guide](/guides/german-family-reunion-language-requirement/) shows that your mission is asking for German language proof, and after [Goethe A1 vs telc A1](/guides/goethe-a1-vs-telc-a1/) has matched that wording to an exact product. The job here is a booking timeline made of confirmed dates. This page does not decide a visa outcome, an exemption, or which certificate your mission accepts.

Settlement and citizenship use the [Germany B1 hub](/germany-b1-settlement-citizenship/). Do not reuse this A1 timeline for those files.

## Decision tree: which date can enter the plan?

Stop when a step is **unknown**. An unknown date is a blank, not a guess.

1. **Is A1 still an open question?** Return to the [requirement guide](/guides/german-family-reunion-language-requirement/). A seat is not an authority answer.
2. **Has your mission named the certificate?** If the words are missing, ask the mission. Compare only named products in [Goethe A1 vs telc A1](/guides/goethe-a1-vs-telc-a1/).
3. **Has the selected centre confirmed a seat for that exact product name?** Record the date, the centre URL or message, and the day you checked it. A different city’s calendar stays out of the plan.
4. **Has that centre confirmed how it publishes the result and how it issues or hands over the certificate?** If it has not, write **unknown**. Do not convert a regulation sentence into a number of days after the exam.
5. **Has the mission confirmed the document deadline or appointment rule for your file?** If it has not, write **unknown**. Do not invent a nationwide “file the certificate within one year” rule. The Federal Foreign Office spouse-language FAQ does not state that rule.
6. **Only then** place a backup seat, and only after the same centre explains its next-seat process. A hoped-for retake date is **unknown** until the centre confirms it.

## What the opened sources support

Checked on 2026-11-10. These points describe process. They are not a visa countdown.

The Federal Foreign Office FAQ says foreign spouses have to prove at least a basic knowledge of German, and it points readers to BAMF and to the German mission in the relevant country. It does not list certificates, fees, result days, or a certificate age.

Residence Act [section 30](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__30.html) is the spouse-of-a-foreigner provision. It includes a simple-German condition and statutory situations where that condition does not apply. It publishes no exam date, no result day, and no certificate day. Whether section 30 is your route is a question for the mission.

The BAMF English leaflet on basic German for spousal reunification (rechecked as reachable on 2026-11-10) names certificates it currently associates with an ALTE-standard proof for the situation it describes, including “Start Deutsch 1” of the Goethe-Institut and “Start Deutsch 1” of telc. It says the German mission where you apply decides whether the proof is recognised. The leaflet is not a calendar.

telc’s English product page for [Start Deutsch 1 / telc Deutsch A1](https://www.telc.net/en/language-examinations/certificate-exams/german/start-german1-telc-german-a1/) (opened 2026-11-10) says the exam has a written part and an oral part and cannot be taken as a partial examination. It states no national fee and no certificate-issue day. An older guessed English path for this product is not the page to use.

telc’s exam regulations, valid from 15 April 2025, say each centre sets registration conditions and prices. They say evaluation at telc starts only when the complete file has arrived, and that the centre must hand the result notice to the candidate within two weeks of **receiving** that notice, where the candidate has met the centre’s contractual duties. That two-week sentence is not a nationwide “certificate N days after the exam” figure. telc’s support FAQ says telc does not publish the exam fee, that the centre must register the session with telc 14 days before the written exam (a centre-to-telc deadline, not your personal booking cutoff), and that written and oral parts on different days may not be more than 7 days apart. Ask the centre which of those rules it has already scheduled for your sitting.

The Goethe-Institut Exam Guidelines (Stand 1 September 2025) say the centre sets the registration deadline, place, and time, and that the fee follows the local fee schedule. They say the exact clock time is communicated in text form about five days before the exam date, and that for a non-modular exam the oral and written parts are usually held within 14 days. Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1 is listed there as a non-modular exam with one overall certificate. The same guidelines say results are generally notified by the centre where you sat the exam, and that an early individual release is not possible. They do not state a national number of days from exam to certificate. Do not turn the “about five days” or “usually 14 days” sentences into buffers on a visa countdown. Confirm both with the centre that is actually booking you.

Goethe product, overview, and centre HTML pages returned curl 403 / fetch 500 in this environment on 2026-11-10. They stay **needs-verify**. The acceptance names above come from the BAMF leaflet, not from those HTML pages.

## Where a regulation sentence must not become a buffer

| Sentence you may see | What it is | What to write in the plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goethe: clock time about five days before the exam | A notice rule in the Exam Guidelines | Confirm the message with your centre. Do not subtract “five days” from a visa appointment. |
| Goethe: non-modular oral and written parts usually within 14 days | When the two parts are held, “in der Regel” | Not a score-release day and not a visa deadline. |
| telc: centre hands over the result notice within two weeks of receiving it | The clock starts when the centre **receives** telc’s notice | Not “two weeks after the exam”. Evaluation waits for a complete file. |
| telc: oral and written parts at most 7 days apart if they fall on different days | A scheduling rule for the centre | Ask which days your sitting uses. |
| A forum, another city, or an unlabelled price PDF | Not your file | Leave the cell **unknown**. |

“telc Deutsch A1 für Zuwanderer” is a separate telc product. The BAMF leaflet’s telc name for this spouse proof is “Start Deutsch 1”. Do not book the other title because the level letter is also A1. The [comparison guide](/guides/goethe-a1-vs-telc-a1/) keeps those names apart.

## FAQ

### Can this page tell me when my certificate will be ready?

No. The result date and the certificate date stay **unknown** until the centre that booked you confirms its current process. The Goethe Exam Guidelines and the telc regulations describe who notifies you and when a centre must pass on a notice it has already received. They do not publish a national number of days after the exam.

### Does the Federal Foreign Office FAQ list accepted certificates or a one-year validity rule?

No. The FAQ opened on 2026-11-10 says foreign spouses have to prove at least a basic knowledge of German, and it sends you to BAMF and to the mission. Certificate names sit in the BAMF leaflet, and recognition sits with your mission. This page does not add a nationwide certificate-age rule.

### Can I use another centre’s fee or waiting time?

No. telc’s FAQ says telc does not publish the exam fee because the centre sets it. Goethe’s Exam Guidelines point to the local fee schedule. Another city’s number stays out of your plan.

### What if I still do not know whether A1 applies?

Do not pay for a seat. Use the [requirement guide](/guides/german-family-reunion-language-requirement/) and the [family-reunion FAQ](/guides/german-a1-family-reunion-faq/) first. This timeline starts after that answer is saved.

## Common mistakes

- Filling an unknown result day with a number from a forum, a different city, or an old draft.
- Treating Goethe’s “about five days” or “usually 14 days” wording as days you can subtract from a visa appointment.
- Treating telc’s “two weeks after the centre receives the result notice” as “the certificate arrives two weeks after the exam”.
- Booking “telc Deutsch A1 für Zuwanderer” because it also says A1.
- Reading a Goethe product HTML page that did not open as if its marketing text had been checked. Those pages are **needs-verify**.
- Starting the timeline before the mission has said that proof is required.
